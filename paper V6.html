Abstract

Challenges in fundamental physics, such as reconciling General Relativity (GR) and Quantum Mechanics (QM) and resolving the measurement problem, indicate potential incompleteness in current frameworks. This paper presents a hardened conceptual interpretation of the six-dimensional (3T+3S) spacetime model by Kletetschka (2025) [1]. We formalize implications for chaos, measurement, and consciousness using mathematical definitions and testable predictions. We hypothesize that 3D time manifests emergent effects akin to two forces: Nedery, a negentropic bias toward complexity, and Mecera, an information-encoding threshold for irreversible measurement. Integrating observable drift from Karve et al. (2025) [2] as a unified chaos signature via decaying correlations, we bridge mathematical descriptions with interpretive implications. The observer is reframed as an evolving system, with consciousness as an advanced ``Navigator'' leveraging these effects. This model is anchored to falsifiable predictions, with added simulations and equations for rigor.
