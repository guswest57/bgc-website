Being a Beast Machine: The Somatic Basis of Selfhood

By Anil Seth

Relevance to our Hypothesis: Seth's work provides a powerful neuroscientific foundation for our concept of the "Navigator." He argues that consciousness is not a high-level thinking process, but is deeply rooted in the body's biological need to stay alive. This aligns perfectly with our idea that the Navigator's primary function is to maintain its own pattern against the forces of Mecera (chaos/entropy) by constantly predicting and regulating its internal state.

The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?

By Karl Friston

Relevance to our Hypothesis: Friston's Free-Energy Principle is the mathematical formalization of the struggle between Nedery (order) and Mecera (chaos). It posits that any self-organizing system, from a single cell to a human brain, must act to minimize "free energy," which can be understood as minimizing surprise or prediction error. This is the fundamental drive of the Navigator: to create an accurate model of the world to maintain its own existence against the constant pressure of the unexpected.

Dissipative adaptation in driven self-assembly

By Jeremy England

Relevance to our Hypothesis: This work from physics offers a compelling origin story for life that mirrors our core concepts. England suggests that life may have arisen not in spite of the Second Law of Thermodynamics (entropy), but because of it. Groups of atoms, when driven by an external energy source (like the sun), will gradually restructure themselves to become better at dissipating that energy. This "dissipative adaptation" is a beautiful physical description of how Nedery can weave patterns of order as a way to more effectively serve the universal tendency towards chaos.
